Saffron Risotto

The dish that teaches you what stirring is for. Twenty-five minutes of attention and a pinch of saffron turn rice and broth into something silky enough to call a course.

F
30 min active 35 min total serves 4 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 1 ½ cups Arborio rice
  • 5 cups Low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 pinch Saffron threads
  • 3 tbsp Unsalted butter
  • 1 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 2 Shallot, minced
  • ½ cup Dry white wine
  • ¾ cup Parmesan cheese, grated
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt
  • ¼ tsp Black pepper

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

With sausage or shrimp

Browned crumbled chicken sausage folded in at the end, or shrimp poached in the last two minutes of broth.

Method

  1. Keep the broth at a bare simmer in a pot beside you. Crush the saffron between your fingers into half a cup of the hot broth and let it steep — it should turn the liquid orange.
  2. Cook the shallots in the oil and 1 tablespoon of butter over medium until soft, 4 minutes. Do not let them color.
  3. Add the rice and toast it 2 minutes, stirring, until the grains look translucent at the edges with a white dot at the center. Never rinse risotto rice; the surface starch is the sauce.
  4. Pour in the wine and stir until it has almost entirely gone.
  5. Now add hot broth a ladleful at a time, stirring steadily, adding more only when the pan bottom stays visible for a moment behind the spoon. About 18 minutes. Add the saffron broth around the halfway mark.
  6. Taste at 16 minutes: the grains should be tender with a firm center. Take it off the heat while it is still slightly looser than you want.
  7. The mantecatura: beat in the last 2 tablespoons of cold butter and the parmesan hard, off the heat, for 30 seconds. It goes from rice-in-broth to glossy and creamy in front of you. Serve immediately, spread flat on warm plates.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
It should spread slowly on the plate, not stand in a mound. Italians call it all'onda — like a wave.
🧒 For the kids
Cheesy rice by another name. Leave the black pepper off theirs and serve it as soon as it is made — risotto waits for nobody.
🥡 Leftovers
Risotto does not reheat well, but leftovers make excellent arancini: chill, roll into balls around a cube of mozzarella, coat in panko, and bake or air-fry at 400°F.
