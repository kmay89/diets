Bean and Avocado Torta

Mexico's great sandwich: a crusty roll, refried beans smeared on one side as glue, avocado, pickled onion and salsa. Fifteen minutes.

D
15 min active 20 min total serves 4 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 1 can Pinto beans, canned no-salt-added, drained
  • 2 tbsp Canola oil
  • 2 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp Ground cumin
  • 1 Red onion, sliced thin
  • 2 Lime
  • ¾ tsp Kosher salt
  • 4 Hoagie rolls
  • 2 Avocado, sliced
  • 3 oz Queso fresco, crumbled
  • ½ cup Salsa
  • 4 leaf Butter lettuce
  • ½ cup Cilantro
  • 1 Jalapeño, sliced (optional)

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Milanesa

A breaded, fried thin chicken cutlet laid over the beans is the classic torta de milanesa.

Method

  1. Quick-pickle the onion: toss the slices with the juice of one lime and 1/4 teaspoon salt and leave them. Twenty minutes turns them pink and sweet.
  2. Fry the garlic and cumin in the oil for 30 seconds, add the beans and 1/4 cup water, and mash them into a thick paste over medium heat, 5 minutes. Season with the rest of the salt.
  3. Split the rolls and toast them cut side down in a dry pan.
  4. Spread the warm beans thickly on the bottom half — they are the glue that keeps everything else in place.
  5. Layer avocado, cheese, salsa, pickled onion, lettuce, cilantro and jalapeño. Press the sandwich down hard before cutting; a torta is supposed to be compressed.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Refried beans as the moisture barrier. It is why a torta survives a lunchbox and a sub does not.
🧒 For the kids
Beans, avocado and cheese only. The pickled onions are usually the sticking point.
🥡 Leftovers
Beans keep 4 days; pickled onions keep 2 weeks and improve everything they touch.
