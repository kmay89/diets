Hoppin' John

Black-eyed peas, rice and smoke — the New Year's Day pot that is supposed to bring luck, and definitely brings a cheap, excellent dinner.

A
15 min active 40 min total serves 6 vegetarianvegan kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 Yellow onion, diced
  • 2 stalks Celery, diced
  • 1 Green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 2 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Dried thyme
  • ½ tsp Red pepper flakes
  • 1 Bay leaves
  • 2 cans Black-eyed peas, canned no-salt-added, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup Brown rice (dry), dry
  • 2 ½ cups Low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 bunch Lacinato kale, stemmed and torn
  • 1 tbsp Red wine vinegar
  • 3 Scallions (green onions), sliced
  • ¾ tsp Kosher salt

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add smoked turkey

Traditional, and it makes the pot non-vegetarian — so brown it separately and add to bowls if that matters at your table.

Method

  1. Cook onion, celery and pepper in the oil over medium for 10 minutes — this trio is the backbone, so let it soften properly.
  2. Add garlic, smoked paprika, thyme and pepper flakes; 1 minute. The smoked paprika is doing the job a ham hock used to.
  3. Add rice, broth, bay leaf and salt. Cover and simmer 30 minutes until the rice is tender.
  4. Fold in the black-eyed peas and kale, cover, and cook 6 minutes more until the greens are silky.
  5. Off heat: vinegar and scallions. The splash of vinegar at the end is traditional and it is what keeps a pot of beans from tasting flat.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
A dash of hot sauce and a pile of scallions per bowl. In the South this is not a garnish, it is the dish.
🧒 For the kids
Serve theirs before the pepper flakes go in, with the greens on the side.
🌿 From the garden
Kale is still standing in the garden on New Year's Day if you mulched it.
🥡 Leftovers
5 days, and it freezes for 3 months. Better on day two.
