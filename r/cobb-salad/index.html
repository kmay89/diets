California Cobb Salad

Invented at the Brown Derby in Hollywood in the 1930s and still the best-composed salad in America. Rows, not a toss — that is the whole idea.

B
25 min active 50 min total serves 4 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 1 head Romaine lettuce, chopped fine
  • 1 head Butter lettuce, chopped
  • 4 Eggs (large), hard-boiled, chopped
  • 2 Avocado, diced
  • 2 cups Cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1 can Chickpeas, canned no-salt-added, drained and roasted crisp
  • 3 oz Feta cheese, crumbled, standing in for blue cheese
  • 3 Scallions (green onions), sliced
  • 5 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp Red wine vinegar
  • 1 Lemon
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 clove Garlic, grated
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt
  • ¼ tsp Black pepper

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

The original: bacon and chicken

Crisp bacon and diced roast chicken breast get their own rows.

Method

  1. Roast the drained chickpeas at 425°F for 25 minutes with a spoonful of oil and a pinch of salt until they crackle. They are the crunch that bacon usually provides.
  2. Chop the lettuces much finer than feels normal. A Cobb is meant to be eaten with a spoon.
  3. Shake the oil, vinegar, lemon juice, mustard, garlic, salt and pepper into a dressing.
  4. Spread the lettuce on a wide platter and lay the toppings in tight parallel rows: egg, avocado, tomato, chickpeas, cheese, scallion.
  5. Bring it to the table looking like that, then dress and toss in front of everyone. That is how the Brown Derby did it and it still gets a reaction.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Everything chopped to the same small dice. It is more knife work and it is the difference between a Cobb and a pile of salad.
🧒 For the kids
Rows mean a child can take exactly what they want and skip the rest without a negotiation. That is the underrated genius of a composed salad.
🥡 Leftovers
Dress only what you are eating. Undressed components keep 3 days and make excellent lunches.
