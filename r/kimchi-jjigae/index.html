Kimchi Jjigae

The stew that exists because kimchi gets old. Sour, red, bubbling, and on the table in half an hour — the most-eaten stew in Korea for a reason.

D
15 min active 35 min total serves 4 vegetarian

Ingredients

  • 2 cups Kimchi, well-fermented, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp Toasted sesame oil
  • 1 Yellow onion, sliced
  • 4 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp Gochugaru (Korean chile flakes)
  • 1 tbsp Gochujang
  • 4 cups Low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 block Extra-firm tofu, cut into thick slabs
  • 1 ½ cups Shiitake mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 tsp Brown sugar
  • 4 Scallions (green onions), cut into 2-inch lengths
  • 1 ½ cups Short-grain sushi rice

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add pork belly or shoulder

The standard version. Fry a few ounces of thinly sliced pork with the kimchi at step 2 and let it render into the base.

Method

  1. Start the rice — jjigae without rice is a strange meal.
  2. Fry the kimchi in the sesame oil over medium-high for 5 minutes with the onion, until the edges caramelize and the smell turns from sharp to sweet. Old, aggressively sour kimchi makes the best jjigae; fresh kimchi makes a thin one.
  3. Add the garlic, gochugaru and gochujang and fry 1 minute more.
  4. Pour in the broth along with any kimchi juice from the jar. Simmer 15 minutes uncovered.
  5. Lay the tofu slabs and mushrooms on top, cover, and simmer 8 minutes without stirring so the tofu stays whole.
  6. Add the sugar to round out the sourness, taste for salt, and scatter the scallions over. Serve bubbling, straight from the pot, with rice alongside.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Frying the kimchi before any liquid goes in is what turns it from sharp to deep. Do not skip the five minutes.
🧒 For the kids
Too sour and too hot for most children. Their portion is better as plain rice, tofu and a fried egg from the same kitchen.
🥡 Leftovers
Improves overnight. Keeps 4 days.
