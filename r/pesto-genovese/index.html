Pesto alla Genovese

Basil, pine nuts, garlic, two cheeses and good oil. Ten minutes, no cooking, and it freezes — the single best thing to do with an August basil plant.

10 min active 10 min total serves 8 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 4 cups Fresh basil, leaves only, packed
  • ⅜ cup Pine nuts
  • 2 cloves Garlic
  • ½ cup Parmesan cheese, grated
  • ¼ cup Pecorino Romano, grated
  • ½ cup Extra-virgin olive oil
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt

Method

  1. Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan over medium for 3 minutes, shaking constantly, until they smell like popcorn and are patched with gold. They burn in seconds.
  2. Pulse the garlic, pine nuts and salt to a paste. Add the basil and pulse in short bursts — long processing heats the leaves and turns them black.
  3. With the machine running, pour in the olive oil in a thin stream.
  4. Pulse in both cheeses last. Pecorino brings the salt and the sharpness; parmesan brings the sweetness. Both is not fussiness, it is the recipe.
  5. Taste for salt. Store with a film of oil on top to keep the surface from browning.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Never heat pesto. Toss it with hot pasta off the burner; cooked pesto turns dull green and loses everything that made it worth making.
🧒 For the kids
Pesto pasta is a reliable dinner. Loosen it with a few spoonfuls of the hot pasta water so it coats rather than clumps.
🌿 From the garden
Pinch the flower buds off your basil all summer and one plant will supply four batches. Basil that has flowered turns bitter.
🥡 Leftovers
Keeps a week under oil in the fridge. Freeze it in an ice cube tray — two cubes is dinner for two in February.
