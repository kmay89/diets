Tiramisu

No baking, no oven, thirty minutes plus a night in the fridge. The two things that matter: hard savoiardi, and a dip in the coffee measured in seconds.

30 min active 390 min total serves 10 vegetarian

Ingredients

  • 24 Ladyfingers (savoiardi)
  • 1 ½ cups Strong brewed coffee or espresso, strong, cooled
  • 1 cup Mascarpone
  • 1 cup Heavy cream
  • 3 Eggs (large), yolks only
  • ½ cup Granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp Vanilla extract
  • 3 tbsp Unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 oz Dark chocolate (70%), shaved (optional)

Method

  1. Whisk the yolks and sugar in a bowl set over barely simmering water for 6-8 minutes, until pale, thick and hot to the touch — about 160°F. This is a zabaglione, and cooking the yolks is what makes the dessert safe as well as stable.
  2. Cool it 10 minutes, then beat in the mascarpone until smooth. Do not overbeat; mascarpone splits.
  3. Whip the cream to soft peaks with the vanilla and fold it in, in three additions.
  4. Dip each ladyfinger in the coffee for one second a side. One. They go on absorbing for twenty minutes afterward, and a soggy tiramisu is always an over-dipped one.
  5. Layer: biscuits, half the cream, biscuits, the rest of the cream. Smooth the top.
  6. Refrigerate at least 6 hours, ideally overnight. Dust thickly with cocoa immediately before serving — cocoa dusted early turns to mud.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
A pinch of salt in the cream. Every rich dessert improves with it and nobody can identify why.
🧒 For the kids
Make a small dish with strong hot chocolate instead of coffee. It works, and it is a genuinely good children's dessert.
🥡 Leftovers
Keeps 3 days and is best on day two. It does not freeze.
