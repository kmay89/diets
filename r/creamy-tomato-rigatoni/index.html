Creamy Tomato Rigatoni with White Beans

The creaminess comes from blended cannellini beans, not cream — which also means 14 grams of protein and 9 grams of fiber a serving. Twenty-five minutes.

A
15 min active 30 min total serves 5 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 12 oz Whole wheat pasta (dry), rigatoni
  • 3 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 Yellow onion, diced
  • 4 cloves Garlic, minced
  • ½ tsp Red pepper flakes
  • 2 tbsp Tomato paste
  • 1 can Crushed tomatoes, no salt added
  • 1 can Cannellini beans, canned no-salt-added, drained and rinsed
  • 4 cups Baby spinach
  • ½ cup Fresh basil, torn
  • 4 tbsp Parmesan cheese, grated
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add chicken sausage or shrimp

Brown one link, sliced, and stir into individual bowls.

Method

  1. Boil the pasta in well-salted water. Reserve 1 cup of pasta water.
  2. Cook the onion in oil over medium 7 minutes; add garlic and pepper flakes 45 seconds; fry the tomato paste 60 seconds until dark.
  3. Add crushed tomatoes and salt; simmer 8 minutes.
  4. Blend the beans with 1/2 cup of the pasta water until completely smooth, then stir into the sauce. It goes from red to a glossy orange-pink — that is the cream you didn't add.
  5. Add spinach and let it wilt, 2 minutes.
  6. Toss the drained pasta into the sauce with a splash of pasta water. Off heat, add basil and parmesan.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Finish the pasta in the sauce for a full minute with pasta water, tossing hard. That emulsion is what makes restaurant pasta cling instead of sit in a puddle.
🧒 For the kids
This is already the recipe most kids ask for again. If yours won't eat visible spinach, wilt it in and blend it with the beans — it disappears completely into the sauce.
🌿 From the garden
Basil, always basil.
🥡 Leftovers
Keeps 4 days. The sauce alone freezes well without the pasta.
