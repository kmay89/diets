Pasta e Ceci

Roman pantry cooking: chickpeas, pasta, rosemary and a lot of olive oil, cooked in one pot until the starch turns the broth silky. Thirty minutes, seven dollars.

A
10 min active 30 min total serves 4 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 4 cloves Garlic, sliced
  • 1 tbsp Fresh rosemary, finely chopped
  • ½ tsp Red pepper flakes
  • 2 tbsp Tomato paste
  • 2 cans Chickpeas, canned no-salt-added, drained and rinsed
  • 4 cups Low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 8 oz Whole wheat pasta (dry), small shape — ditalini or shells
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt
  • 4 tbsp Parmesan cheese, grated
  • ¼ cup Flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • 1 tbsp juice Lemon

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add tuna or pancetta-style crisp

A drained can of good tuna flaked into the omnivore bowls is traditional along the Italian coast and takes zero cooking.

Method

  1. Warm the oil, garlic, rosemary and pepper flakes together over medium — start them in cold oil so the garlic perfumes the fat instead of burning. 3 minutes.
  2. Add tomato paste, fry 60 seconds. Add one can of chickpeas and mash them roughly with a wooden spoon.
  3. Add the second can, the broth and salt. Simmer 10 minutes.
  4. Add the dry pasta directly to the pot and cook, stirring often, until al dente — 9-11 minutes. The pasta starch is what makes the broth silky, so do not boil it separately.
  5. Off heat, stir in parmesan and lemon juice, and let it sit 3 minutes to thicken. Finish with parsley and a hard pour of raw olive oil over each bowl.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Finishing oil poured raw over the bowl — a full teaspoon each. It smells like a trattoria and it is the healthiest fat in the kitchen.
🧒 For the kids
Use a fun small pasta shape and go light on the pepper flakes. Most kids treat this as 'bean soup with noodles' and eat two bowls.
🌿 From the garden
Rosemary in a pot by the door, dragged inside in November.
🥡 Leftovers
The pasta drinks the broth overnight. Add half a cup of water when reheating.
