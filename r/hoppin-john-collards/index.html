Hoppin' John with Collards

Black-eyed peas and brown rice with the greens cooked right into the pot — peas for coins, greens for folding money. West African in origin and eaten across the Carolinas on New Year's Day.

A
20 min active 50 min total serves 6 vegetarianvegan kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 Yellow onion, diced
  • 3 stalks Celery, diced
  • 1 Green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 2 tsp Smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Dried thyme
  • 2 Bay leaves
  • ¼ tsp Cayenne pepper
  • 2 cans Black-eyed peas, canned no-salt-added, drained
  • 1 ½ cups Brown rice (dry)
  • 3 ½ cups Low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp Kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp Apple cider vinegar
  • 6 cups Collard greens, sliced into ribbons
  • 4 Scallions (green onions), sliced
  • 2 tsp Louisiana-style hot sauce

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Smoked ham or bacon

Two slices of bacon rendered at the start, or a cup of diced ham added with the beans.

Method

  1. Cook the onion, celery and bell pepper in the oil over medium for 8 minutes. That trio is the Southern trinity and it starts most of what follows in this file.
  2. Add the garlic, paprika, thyme, bay and cayenne and cook 1 minute — the smoked paprika is standing in for the ham hock, and it does the job.
  3. Add the rice, broth and salt. Bring to a boil, cover, and cook on the lowest heat 35 minutes for brown rice.
  4. Stir in the black-eyed peas and the collard ribbons, cover, and leave off the heat 10 minutes. The greens wilt in the residual steam without going gray.
  5. Fluff with a fork, add the vinegar, and taste for salt. Scallions and hot sauce at the table.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
The vinegar at the end. Southern bean cookery almost always finishes with acid, and it is the step that gets left out of written recipes.
🧒 For the kids
Rice with beans in it. Chop the collards fine so they read as flecks rather than as greens.
🥡 Leftovers
Keeps 5 days. Fry leftovers into a crisp cake in a hot skillet.
