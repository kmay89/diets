Mushroom Paprikash

Hungarian sweet paprika by the tablespoon, off the heat so it never scorches, finished with sour cream and served over buttered egg noodles.

D
25 min active 45 min total serves 5 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 6 cups Cremini mushrooms, thickly sliced
  • 2 Portobello mushrooms, cut into chunks
  • 2 tbsp Canola oil
  • 2 tbsp Unsalted butter
  • 2 Yellow onion, sliced thin
  • 1 Red bell pepper, sliced
  • 4 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp Smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Chili powder
  • 2 tbsp All-purpose flour
  • 2 ½ cups Low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 tbsp Tomato paste
  • ¾ cup Light sour cream
  • 1 ¼ tsp Kosher salt
  • 10 oz Semolina pasta (dry), egg noodles
  • 3 tbsp Fresh dill, chopped

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Chicken paprikash

Bone-in thighs browned first and simmered 35 minutes in the sauce. It is the original and the method is identical.

Method

  1. Brown the mushrooms hard in the oil in two batches, 6 minutes each, and set them aside.
  2. Cook the onions and pepper in the butter over medium for 12 minutes until soft and sweet.
  3. Take the pot off the heat before adding the paprika. Paprika burns in about fifteen seconds over direct heat and turns bitter — Hungarian cooks always add it off the flame.
  4. Stir in the flour and tomato paste, then return to medium heat and add the broth gradually, whisking. Return the mushrooms and simmer 15 minutes.
  5. Off the heat, temper the sour cream: stir a ladle of the hot sauce into it first, then stir that back into the pot. Straight from the tub into a simmering pot it will curdle.
  6. Serve over buttered noodles with dill.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Buy good Hungarian sweet paprika in a tin and keep it in the freezer. It is a completely different ingredient from the dusty jar in most cupboards.
🧒 For the kids
Mild, creamy and served on noodles. Skip the dill on theirs if herbs are an issue.
🥡 Leftovers
Keeps 4 days. Reheat gently — sour cream splits at a boil.
