Garden Frittata

Breakfast for dinner, built on whatever the garden gave you. Extra whites keep it light without making it taste like a diet.

F
15 min active 35 min total serves 4 vegetarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 6 Eggs (large)
  • 4 Egg whites
  • ¼ cup 1% milk
  • 2 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 Yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 Zucchini, half-moons
  • 1 cup Cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 3 cups Baby spinach
  • 2 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 2 oz Goat cheese (chèvre), crumbled
  • ¼ cup Fresh basil, torn
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt
  • ½ tsp Black pepper
  • 4 slices Whole grain bread, toasted

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add crumbled chicken sausage

Brown it with the onions if the whole pan can be non-vegetarian; otherwise scatter it on one half after the eggs go in.

Method

  1. Heat oven to 375°F. Beat eggs, whites, milk, salt and pepper until completely uniform — 30 seconds of real whisking, not ten stirs.
  2. Cook the onion in oil in an oven-safe skillet over medium 6 minutes; add zucchini and cook 5 more until it browns at the edges.
  3. Add garlic and spinach; cook until the spinach wilts and any water has evaporated. Wet vegetables make a weeping frittata.
  4. Pour in the eggs, scatter the tomatoes and goat cheese, and cook undisturbed 3 minutes until the edges set.
  5. Transfer to the oven for 12-14 minutes until the center is just barely set — it keeps cooking out of the oven.
  6. Rest 5 minutes, then slide onto a board, top with basil, and cut into wedges. Serve with toast.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Pull it from the oven while the center still wobbles slightly. Overcooked frittata is rubbery and everyone has eaten one.
🧒 For the kids
A frittata is just eggs. Serve a wedge with toast and fruit and most kids are happy.
🌿 From the garden
Precisely the recipe for a Tuesday in August when there are three zucchini on the counter.
🥡 Leftovers
Excellent cold or at room temperature, which makes it a lunchbox and a picnic item too.
