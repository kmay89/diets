Roast Salmon with Blackberry and Hazelnut

The Pacific Northwest on a plate: salmon roasted low and slow, a sharp blackberry pan sauce, and toasted Oregon hazelnuts. Thirty minutes.

C
15 min active 35 min total serves 4 pescatarian kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 4 fillets Salmon fillet
  • 2 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • ¾ tsp Kosher salt
  • 1 ½ cups Blackberries
  • 1 Shallot, minced
  • 2 tbsp Balsamic vinegar
  • 1 tbsp Pure maple syrup
  • 3 sprigs Fresh thyme
  • 1 tbsp Unsalted butter
  • ⅜ cup Hazelnuts, toasted and chopped
  • 1 cup Farro (dry)
  • 4 cups Arugula

🌱 Fork in the road — for the vegetarian

Roasted portobellos

Two large portobellos per person, roasted at 425°F for 20 minutes, take the same sauce beautifully.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 275°F. Salmon roasted low comes out silky and evenly cooked all the way through, and it is very hard to overshoot.
  2. Cook the farro in salted water, 25 minutes, and drain.
  3. Rub the salmon with oil and salt and roast 18-22 minutes, until it flakes with gentle pressure and reads 125°F at the thickest point.
  4. Meanwhile, simmer the blackberries, shallot, balsamic, maple and thyme in a small pan for 8 minutes, crushing half the berries. Strain it if you want it smooth; leave it if you want it rustic.
  5. Off the heat, swirl the butter into the sauce so it turns glossy.
  6. Serve the salmon on the farro with arugula alongside, the sauce spooned over, and the hazelnuts scattered last so they stay crisp.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
Butter swirled into a sauce off the heat — a monter au beurre. One tablespoon turns a thin fruit reduction into something that clings.
🧒 For the kids
Serve the sauce on the side. Salmon and buttery grains with berries to dip into is usually a win.
🥡 Leftovers
Salmon keeps 2 days and is very good cold, flaked into the leftover farro with more arugula.
