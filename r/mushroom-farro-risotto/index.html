Mushroom Farro "Risotto"

All the comfort of risotto with a chewy whole grain and none of the standing-and-stirring. Deeply savory from properly browned mushrooms.

C
20 min active 45 min total serves 4 vegetarian

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • 16 oz Cremini mushrooms, quartered
  • 2 Shallot, minced
  • 3 cloves Garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp Fresh thyme, leaves
  • 1 ½ cups Farro (dry), rinsed
  • ½ cup Dry white wine
  • 4 cups Low-sodium vegetable broth, kept warm
  • 5 tbsp Parmesan cheese, finely grated
  • 1 tbsp Unsalted butter
  • 1 tsp zest Lemon
  • ¼ cup Flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • ¾ tsp Kosher salt
  • ½ tsp Black pepper

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add seared steak or a fried egg

Sliced sirloin over the top turns this into a restaurant entrée; a crispy fried egg does the same job for less money.

Method

  1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a wide skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Add the mushrooms in a single layer and — this is the important part — do not touch them or salt them for 4 minutes. Let them brown, then toss and brown 4 more minutes. Season, remove, set aside.
  2. Lower the heat to medium. Add the last tablespoon of oil, the shallots and a pinch of salt; cook 3 minutes. Add garlic and thyme, 45 seconds.
  3. Add farro and toast it in the fat for 2 minutes until it smells nutty.
  4. Pour in the wine and let it cook away completely, scraping the bottom.
  5. Add warm broth a cup at a time, stirring occasionally and adding more as it absorbs — about 30 minutes total. Farro is forgiving; it does not need constant attention like arborio.
  6. When the grains are tender with a little chew, stir the mushrooms back in with parmesan, butter and lemon zest. It should be loose enough to spread slowly on the plate. Finish with parsley and a lot of black pepper.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
The lemon zest at the end is what makes people ask what's in it. Do not substitute juice — the oil in the zest is the point.
🧒 For the kids
Most kids will eat the farro plain with parmesan. Set a portion aside before the mushrooms go back in.
🌿 From the garden
Thyme is a perennial here; one plant survives Ohio winters and you snip from it for years.
🥡 Leftovers
Press cold leftovers into patties and pan-fry in olive oil for arancini-adjacent lunch.
