Build-a-Bowl (the Formula)

Not a recipe so much as an equation: grain plus roasted vegetable plus protein plus sauce plus crunch plus acid. Learn it once and you never need a recipe for lunch again.

A
15 min active 40 min total serves 4 vegetarianvegan kids eat it

Ingredients

  • 1 cup Farro (dry), dry — or quinoa, or brown rice
  • 1 head Broccoli, florets — or any roastable vegetable
  • 2 Sweet potato, diced
  • 1 can Chickpeas, canned no-salt-added, drained and dried
  • 3 tbsp Extra-virgin olive oil
  • ¼ cup Tahini
  • 3 tbsp juice Lemon
  • 1 clove Garlic, grated
  • 4 tbsp Pumpkin seeds (pepitas)
  • ½ cup Flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • ½ Red onion, thinly sliced and quick-pickled
  • ½ tsp Black pepper
  • ½ tsp Kosher salt

🍽 Fork in the road — for the omnivores

Add any leftover protein

Sliced chicken, salmon, a jammy egg — the formula does not care.

Method

  1. GRAIN: cook 1 cup dry farro like pasta in salted water, 25 minutes, drain. Makes about 3 cups.
  2. ROASTED: toss vegetables and dried chickpeas with 2 tbsp oil and salt; roast at 425°F for 25-30 minutes.
  3. SAUCE: whisk tahini, lemon, garlic, a pinch of salt and cold water until pourable.
  4. PICKLE: cover the sliced onion with red wine vinegar and a pinch of salt while everything roasts. Twenty minutes is enough.
  5. CRUNCH: toasted pepitas, or toasted panko, or the crispy chickpeas.
  6. ASSEMBLE in that order — grain, roasted things, sauce, pickle, crunch, herbs — and finish with black pepper and the last of the olive oil.
  7. The rule: every bowl needs something soft, something crisp, something acidic, and something fatty. If a bowl tastes boring, it is missing acid.

Notes

✨ The restaurant touch
The quick pickle. Every good grain bowl in every good restaurant has one, and it takes twenty minutes of doing nothing.
🧒 For the kids
Same components, arranged in separate piles rather than layered, with sauce in a small cup.
🌿 From the garden
Whatever is ripe goes in the roasted slot.
🥡 Leftovers
Components keep 4-5 days separately. This is how Sunday cooking becomes four lunches.
